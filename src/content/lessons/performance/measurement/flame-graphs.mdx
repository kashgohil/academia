A sampling profiler collects thousands of stacks. Brendan Gregg's **flame graph** (2011) draws them all at once:

- each box is a function in a stack,
- its **width** is the share of samples in which that stack prefix appeared (inclusive time),
- frames are stacked by call depth, callers below callees,
- functions with nothing above them, the *top edge*, are where the CPU actually was (self time).

1. Find the widest **plateau**: a wide box with little stacked on top of it is self time. Here, `socket_read` under `db_query`.
2. Type **alloc** into the highlight box. `alloc_string` appears under both `parse_json` and `decode_rows`: total allocation cost is the sum of separate towers. Profiler UIs offer a merged "by function" view for exactly this.
3. Click **render_template** to zoom in: `hash_key` and `escape_html` are its children. Click a frame below it to zoom out.
4. Switch to **icicle** (root at the top), the layout Chrome DevTools and speedscope's left-heavy view use. Same data, flipped.

## Reading strategy

<Steps>
<div>Look at the top edge for wide boxes: they're where cycles are spent.</div>
<div>Follow a wide tower down to see <i>why</i> it's called: which caller path is responsible. Often the fix is in the caller (call it less), not the leaf (make it faster).</div>
<div>Look for the same function in several towers. Spread-out costs like allocation, locking or logging hide in flame graphs, so use search, or a function-merged view.</div>
<div>Ignore colors in classic flame graphs: they are random warm hues, chosen to separate neighbors. Some tools color by meaning instead (kernel vs user, language, or the change in a differential graph).</div>
</Steps>

## Variants

| Chart | x axis | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| Flame graph | merged samples, sorted | where CPU time goes overall |
| Flame chart (DevTools, Tracy, Perfetto) | time | what happened when; latency of one request |
| Off-CPU flame graph | merged blocked time | where threads wait (I/O, locks) |
| Differential flame graph | merged, colored by change | what got slower between two profiles |
| Memory flame graph | merged allocation sizes | who allocates |

Getting one: `perf record -g ./app` then `perf script | stackcollapse-perf.pl | flamegraph.pl > out.svg`, or `cargo flamegraph`, `samply record`, `go tool pprof -http=:8080`, or load a profile into speedscope.
